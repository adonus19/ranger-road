# Ranger's Road Program

## Duration

9 months / 36 weeks.

Each month is one chapter with a named trial.

## Core Weekly Rhythm

Most chapters use:
- Monday: strength
- Tuesday: road/conditioning
- Wednesday: restoration or skill
- Thursday: strength
- Friday: short conditioning/restoration
- Saturday: quest / long road / trial preparation
- Sunday: rest, worship, reflection

The [full program content pack](rangers-road-full-program-content/PROGRAM_INDEX.md) contains the dated work and detailed prescriptions. For Chapter I, use [The Muster](rangers-road-full-program-content/01_THE_MUSTER.md) for Forge A/B, walking doses, restoration, Scripture, and the Gate Trial.

Chapter I has four complete Monday–Sunday weeks. When the chosen Day 1 is Tuesday through Sunday, the days from Day 1 through the first Sunday are a short lead-in: use Week 1's existing content for each matching weekday, then begin the full Week 1 on Monday. A Monday Day 1 begins Week 1 immediately. The lead-in adds no new progression step or trial. While the Gate Trial remains pending after Week 4, continue the Week 4 weekday template.

Chapter I is acknowledged as complete on the day after the fourth full week ends, if a completed Gate Trial result is in local history. Completing the Gate Trial earlier does not skip the remaining dated orders through Week 4 Sunday. This acknowledgment does not automatically start Chapter II.

For Week 4 Forge B, the documented reduction of about 25% means completing around 15 of the standard 20 work sets. The person chooses about five sets to skip and records those skips; the base Forge B prescription remains available as a historical snapshot. Do not automatically add load or make the deload harder on a Yellow or pain-affected day.

Only 3 days should feel like major commitments.
Two optional/support days should generally stay near 30 minutes.

In Chapter I, Wednesday skill practice means nonexertional tool inspection or basic knots. It does not need a training readiness check. Restoration and any exertional skill work do require the check.

## Readiness Check

Before training:
- sleep hours
- whether sleep felt poor
- energy 1–5
- back pain 0–10
- shoulder pain 0–10
- neck pain 0–10

### Green
Energy >= 3 and pain 0–2.
Normal mission.

### Yellow
Poor sleep, energy 2, or pain 3–4.
Reduce volume ~25%; do not increase load.

Poor sleep is self-reported; sleep hours alone do not set a fixed readiness threshold.

### Red
Energy 1, pain 5–10, significant symptom increase, new neurological/radiating symptoms, illness, or other concerning symptoms.
No strength mission. Use restoration/easy movement only if appropriate.

A completed Gate Trial uses the full documented 2-mile walk and 3-round circuit, so it waits for a Green readiness day; on Yellow or Red, defer the full trial rather than record it as completed.

## Global Training Rules

- Never miss twice.
- No hero workouts.
- Do not compensate for missed work with excessive volume.
- Progress only when technique is good and symptoms are stable.
- Do not auto-progress after a yellow/red session.
- Running is not required early.
- Heavy deadlifting is not part of the default program.
- Rucking begins conservatively.
- Strength is trained for usefulness, not 1RM status.

## Strength Progression

Typical rule:
- if technique good
- no meaningful symptom increase
- final set <= RPE 7

Then next exposure may progress.

Typical changes:
- upper-body barbell: +5 lb
- squat: +5 lb or reduced box height
- dumbbell: reps first, then load
- pull-up: reduce band assistance when 3 clean sets at top range are achieved

## Measurements

Day 1 and monthly:

- resting heart rate

### Check-ins

- A check-in is due on Day 1 and every 28 days after (Days 1, 29, 57, …). Its cadence stays anchored to Day 1 even when a short Chapter I lead-in moves the end of Week 4. It can be saved any day within its 28 days.
- Weight and waist can also be logged on their own on any day.
- A check-in needs weight (lb), waist (in), and resting heart rate (bpm). Blood pressure and the four tests are optional.
- Before taking any of the four tests, record a same-day readiness check. A body-only check-in can be saved without one. On a Red day, hold the tests until another day with a non-Red readiness check.
- Average sleep and pain trends are not typed. They are averaged from the readiness checks of the 28 days ending on the check-in date, counting the latest check of each day, and saved with the check-in so later checks cannot change it.
- Energy and perceived capability are rated 1–5, as in the readiness check.
- On a Red readiness day the four tests (push-ups, pull-up assistance, squat depth, toe reach) wait for another day. Body measurements can still be saved, and the held tests can be added later in the same 28 days.

How each measure is taken:
- Resting heart rate: sit quietly for 5 minutes, then count for 60 seconds.
- Blood pressure: from a home cuff, if one is available.
- Push-ups: one set; stop at the first rep with broken form or rising pain.
- Pull-up assistance: the band used for clean reps, or none.
- Squat depth: in words (above parallel, parallel, below parallel); how low the person can sit back with good form and no pain.
- Toe reach: stand tall with knees straight and reach down slowly; inches from fingertips to the floor, 0 when they touch it.
- Stop any test at the first sign of rising pain.

Major missions also track:
- distance
- duration
- terrain
- load
- RPE
- recovery
- post-mission function
